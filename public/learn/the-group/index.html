The Group

Investors who share the good seed deals they find, decide alone and invest alone. Stag Hunt never touches money, never invests for anyone, and takes no fees or share of profits.

Started by Jonathan Manta

A deal anyone could find is a hare. A deal only you could find is how we catch the stag.

Who's in the group

No cap. Each investor who joins may bring deals the others would not have seen.

Why

Network effects. Each investor who joins may bring deals the others would not have seen, so the group can be worth more to each member as it grows.

Three kinds of investor make a good mix. Connectors tend to meet founders early. Builders have built and sold a company. Specialists know one field deeply.

Why

Comparative advantage. People with different knowledge see different deals and catch different flaws. Ten people who know the same things are worth little more than one.

Members may share a deal when they find one worth backing. No quotas. The game behind it

Why

When a number becomes a target, people tend to hit the number instead of the goal. A quota would likely bring more deals and worse ones.

How it's different

Stag Hunt is its own kind of investing. You decide and invest like a solo angel, with no fees and every dollar yours. But you can see deals the way a fund does, because members share theirs.

Why

Funds exist because single investors couldn't see enough deals, and they charge for that access with fees and with control over your money. Stag Hunt aims to get the access through cooperation instead, so you keep both.

As a member

Money
Any check size
Why

Matching. A deal you can't afford is noise, and a yes from someone who can't invest says little. Showing each member the deals that fit cuts search costs and helps keep signals credible.

Deals
Shares good deals, vouches for the founders and makes the introduction
Why

This is the stag hunt itself. Sharing your best deal costs you little, and it can earn you the others'. When members can see who shares, cooperating tends to become the stable choice. A vouch from someone who knows the team can do the work that weeks of meetings usually do, and their name on the record helps keep the vouch honest.

Yes or no
Yes or no to meeting the team, before seeing anyone else's call. Diligence follows on your own clock
Why

Information cascades. Once people see others' answers they tend to copy them, and the group learns little. A crowd tends to be wise when each judgment is independent. A first read in about a week helps, because good rounds can close fast. A yes means you want to meet the founders. Early investors back teams, and it is hard to know a team from a page, so the member who brought the deal makes the introduction.

Standards
No gambling, adult content or cheating
Why

Reputation is shared. One company built on harm can lower the standing of the whole group, a cost the poster doesn't pay alone. So the line is the same for each member.

Discretion
Deals and terms stay among members
Why

Founders share what they would not post publicly because they trust it goes no further. Break that trust and good founders may stop coming.

Conflicts
No pay or perks from companies shared here
Why

Incentives. A member paid to bring a deal is an agent for the company, not for the group, and their yes stops meaning what it says.

Rules
Follows the law and their employer's rules
Why

One member's breach can put the others at risk. Rules that protect the group have to bind each member first.

Time
As much as you want. A call can take about a minute
Why

The cheaper it is to take part, the more people can, and the group can get more valuable with each member who does.

What you get

Deals
Deals other members find and share, not just your own
Why

The power law. In seed investing a few companies make most of the money, so one way to raise your odds of owning one is to see more good deals than you could find alone.

Opinions
A yes or no from members a deal fits
Why

People who see the world differently make different mistakes. Their independent judgments, added together, can cancel out much of the error in any one of them.

Record
A record of who called it right
Why

This is a repeated game. When calls are on the record, good judgment and honest sharing can build a reputation, and that reputation can be worth more than a single deal.

Your call
You decide alone. No fees
Why

No one invests on your behalf, so no one else's incentives come between you and your money.

What we back

Who
Honest US founders raising a first round
Why

The first round is where outsiders tend to know the least and a trusted network can add the most. Honesty first, because the other numbers a founder gives you depend on it.

Potential
Could return 10x or more
Why

Most startups fail. For a portfolio to work, each company has to be able to pay for the ones that don't.

Never
Gambling, adult content or cheating people
Why

A business that wins only when its customers lose takes value instead of creating it. We back builders.

Character
Worth checking with people who know them well
Why

A founder knows more about themselves than an investor can. People who have worked with them can help close that gap.

Getting into the round

Who posts it
Stays the main contact and asks for a spot
Why

One point of contact keeps the founder's cost low and makes one member accountable for the relationship.

If it's full
The founder picks who gets in
Why

It's their company. Respecting their choice is part of how good founders keep coming back.

No bargaining
The group never bargains as one
Why

Each member decides and invests alone. Acting as one buyer would turn independent investors into something closer to a fund.

How we play fair

Be upfront
Say what you own. Never call your own deal
Why

A poster's yes is not independent. Saying what you own lets everyone weigh your view for what it is.

Be honest
Share the weak points, not just the strong
Why

Praise is free to give, so on its own it says little. Members trust what's shared here because the bad news is shared too.

Look further
Bring founders from outside your circle
Why

Your circle tends to see the same deals you do. The value is in what others don't see.

Mistakes
An honest slip gets a talk. A leak means leaving
Why

Cooperation lasts when the response fits the intent: forgive honest mistakes, end deliberate betrayal. Too soft and trust erodes; too harsh and people stop daring to take part.

Jonathan's role

His power
No say over anyone's call. He plays the Stag Hunt like the other members
Why

A game is fair when no player can tilt it. Jonathan can't change anyone's call or push a deal, and the rules apply to him the same as to other members. As the site's admin, he can remove what breaks the rules.

Calls
His yes or no counts once, like yours. He sees others’ only after making his own
Why

Independent judgments add up when no one, including the founder of the site, can see the answers first.

His money
His own, in his own name, like other members. No fee from any company
Why

Same stake, same terms as other members. He does well when the deals he backs do well, and he takes nothing from companies, media or advertisers.

His job
Runs the site. He doesn't pick who joins
Why

The game can get better with each investor who plays. An open door lets it grow, and your record of calls, not a gatekeeper, is what earns trust here.

Backup
A second admin can run the group without him
Why

No single point of failure. The group shouldn't depend on any one person, including its founder.

References
On request: people who have built with him
Why

Trust should be checkable. Look into him the way you would look into a founder.

How to join

1 · Sign up
Enter your email. No invite, no call, no fee
Why

Each investor who plays can make the game better for the rest, so the door is open.

2 · Check size
Set the most you'd put in one deal
Why

So the deals you see are ones you can actually invest in.

3 · Play

Why

Trust here is earned in the open. Your record of calls is what members look at, not who let you in.

Look into us
Meet members and look into Jonathan
Why

Trust runs both ways. Check us as carefully as you would a founder.

Stag Hunt is a new way to invest: investors share the good early-stage deals they find, and each decides alone. It is not a fund, broker-dealer, funding portal or investment adviser, and it does not offer, sell or recommend securities. It never pools, holds or moves money. Neither Stag Hunt nor its organizer or members take fees or compensation from any company discussed. Members' calls and comments are opinions, not investment advice. Every investment is made directly between a member and a company, on that company's own terms and documents. Startup investing is illiquid and high-risk; most startups fail.
