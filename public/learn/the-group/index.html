The Group

Investors who share every good seed deal they find, decide alone and invest alone. Stag Hunt never touches money, never invests for anyone, and takes no fees or share of profits.

Started by Jonathan Manta

A deal anyone could find is a hare. A deal only you could find is how we catch the stag.

Who's in the group

No cap. Every investor who joins brings deals the others would never see.

Why

Network effects. Every investor who joins brings deals the others would never have seen, so the group is worth more to each member with every person who joins.

Three kinds of investor make the best mix. Connectors meet founders first. Builders have built and sold a company. Specialists know one field better than anyone outside it.

Why

Comparative advantage. People with different knowledge see different deals and catch different flaws. Ten people who know the same things are worth little more than one.

Members share a deal whenever they find one worth backing. No quotas. The game behind it

Why

When a number becomes a target, people hit the number instead of the goal. A quota would bring more deals and worse ones.

How it's different

Stag Hunt is its own kind of investing. You decide and invest like a solo angel, with no fees and every dollar yours. But you see deals the way a fund does, because every member brings theirs.

Why

Funds exist because single investors couldn't see enough deals, and they charge for that access with fees and with control over your money. Stag Hunt gets the access through cooperation instead, so you keep both.

Every member

Money
Any check size
Why

Matching. A deal you can't afford is noise, and a yes from someone who can't invest means nothing. Showing each member only the deals that fit cuts search costs and keeps every signal credible.

Deals
Shares every good deal, vouches for the founders and makes the introduction
Why

This is the stag hunt itself. Sharing your best deal costs you little, and it earns you everyone else's. When everyone can see who shares, cooperating becomes the stable choice. A vouch from someone who knows the team does the work that weeks of meetings usually do, and their name on the record keeps the vouch honest.

Yes or no
Yes or no to meeting the team within 7 days, before seeing anyone else's call. Diligence follows on your own clock
Why

Information cascades. Once people see others' answers they tend to copy them, and the group learns nothing new. A crowd is only wise when each judgment is independent. Seven days is for a first read, because good rounds close fast. A yes means you want to meet the founders. Early investors back teams, and nobody knows a team from a page, so the member who brought the deal makes the introduction.

Standards
No gambling, adult content or cheating
Why

Reputation is shared. One company built on harm lowers the standing of every member, a cost the poster doesn't pay alone. So the line is drawn for everyone.

Discretion
Deals and terms stay among members
Why

Founders share what they would never post publicly because they trust it goes no further. Break that trust and the best founders stop coming, leaving only the ones with nowhere else to go.

Conflicts
No pay or perks from companies shared here
Why

Incentives. A member paid to bring a deal is an agent for the company, not for the group, and their yes stops meaning what it says.

Rules
Follows the law and their employer's rules
Why

One member's breach puts everyone at risk. Rules that protect the group have to bind each member first.

Time
As much as you want. A call takes a minute
Why

The cheaper it is to take part, the more people can, and the group gets more valuable with every member who does.

What you get

Deals
The best deals every member finds, not just your own
Why

The power law. In seed investing a few companies make almost all the money, so the surest way to own one is to see more good deals than you could find alone.

Opinions
An honest yes or no from every member a deal fits
Why

People who see the world differently make different mistakes. Their independent judgments, added together, cancel out much of the error in any one of them.

Record
A permanent record of who called it right
Why

This is a repeated game. When every call is on the record, good judgment and honest sharing build a reputation, and that reputation is worth more than any single deal.

Your call
You decide alone. No fees, no pressure
Why

No one invests on your behalf, so no one else's incentives come between you and your money.

What we back

Who
Honest US founders raising a first round
Why

The first round is where outsiders know the least and a trusted network adds the most. Honesty first, because every other number a founder gives you depends on it.

Potential
Could return 10x or more
Why

Most startups fail. For a portfolio to work, each company has to be able to pay for the ones that don't.

Never
Gambling, adult content or cheating people
Why

A business that wins only when its customers lose takes value instead of creating it. We back builders.

Character
Checked with people who know them well
Why

A founder knows more about themselves than any investor can. People who have worked with them close that gap.

Getting into the round

Who posts it
Stays the main contact and asks for a spot
Why

One point of contact keeps the founder's cost low and makes one member accountable for the relationship.

If it's full
The founder picks who gets in
Why

It's their company. Respecting their choice is how the best founders keep coming back.

No bargaining
The group never bargains as one
Why

Each member decides and invests alone. Acting as one buyer would turn independent investors into something closer to a fund.

How we play fair

Be upfront
Say what you own. Never call your own deal
Why

A poster's yes is not independent. Saying what you own lets everyone weigh your view for what it is.

Founders
An answer within two weeks. Always reply
Why

A slow no costs a founder more than a fast one. How we treat founders today decides which founders come to us tomorrow.

Be honest
Share the weak points, not just the strong
Why

Praise is free to give, so on its own it says little. Members trust what's shared here because the bad news is shared too.

Look further
Bring founders from outside your circle
Why

Your circle sees the same deals you do. The value is in what others can't see.

Mistakes
An honest slip gets a talk. A leak means leaving
Why

Cooperation lasts when the response fits the intent: forgive honest mistakes, end deliberate betrayal. Too soft and trust erodes; too harsh and no one dares take part.

Jonathan's role

His power
None. He plays the Stag Hunt like every other member
Why

A game is only fair if no player can tilt it. Jonathan can't overrule a call or push a deal, and every rule applies to him the same as to everyone.

Calls
His yes or no counts once, like yours. He sees others’ only after making his own
Why

Independent judgments only add up if no one, including the founder of the site, can see the answers first.

His money
His own, in his own name, like any member. Never a fee from any company
Why

Same stake, same terms as everyone. He does well only when the deals members back do well, and nothing comes from companies, media or advertisers.

His job
Runs the site. He doesn't pick who joins
Why

The game gets better with every investor who plays. An open door lets it grow, and your record of calls, not a gatekeeper, is what earns trust here.

Backup
A second admin can run the group without him
Why

No single point of failure. The group shouldn't depend on any one person, including its founder.

References
On request: people who have built with him
Why

Trust should be checkable. Look into him the way you would look into a founder.

How to join

1 · Sign up
Enter your email. No invite, no call, no fee
Why

Every investor who plays makes the game better for the rest, so nothing stands at the door.

2 · Check size

Why

So you see deals you can actually invest in from your first day.

3 · Play

Look into us

Why

Trust runs both ways. Check us as carefully as you would a founder.

Stag Hunt is a new way to invest: investors share every good early-stage deal they find, and each decides alone. It is not a fund, broker-dealer, funding portal or investment adviser, and it does not offer, sell or recommend securities. It never pools, holds or moves money. Neither Stag Hunt nor its organizer or members take fees or compensation from any company discussed. Members' calls and comments are opinions, not investment advice. Every investment is made directly between a member and a company, on that company's own terms and documents. Startup investing is illiquid and high-risk; most startups fail.
