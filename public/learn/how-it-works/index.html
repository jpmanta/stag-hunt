Using Stag Hunt

How it works

Investors share deals. Founders raise their round.

It's free for both. Pick your side to see your steps.

Go to the steps for investors or for founders.

Join

1

Step 1. Sign up with your email

Enter your email and we send you a sign-in link. Your first sign-in makes your account. No invite, no call, no fee.

Why

Each investor who plays can make the game better for the rest, so the door is open.

2

Step 2. Say your check size

Give your name and the largest check you'd write. A check is the amount you put into one company. You'll see deals that take a check that size or smaller. Leave it empty to see every deal.

Why

Matching. A deal you can't afford is noise, and a yes from someone who can't invest says little. Showing each member the deals that fit cuts search costs and helps keep signals credible.

3

Step 3. Make your investor statement

Before you see any deal, you say in writing whether you're an accredited investor, and you promise to keep deals confidential.

Accredited generally means $200,000 in yearly income ($300,000 with a spouse or partner), $1 million in net worth not counting your home, or a Series 7, 65 or 82 license.

If you're accredited, other members' deals open for you 30 days after your statement.

If you're not, you can still bring deals, start hunts and build your record. You won't see other members' deals.

Why

Most private rounds are open only to accredited investors. The 30-day wait means every round reaches only people Stag Hunt already knows.

Share

4

Step 4. Join or start a hunt

A hunt is a group that shares deals. Every new member starts in the same first hunt. You can start your own with the people you already trade deals with, and invite them by email. Joining is their choice.

5

Step 5. Share a deal

When you meet a founder worth backing, share the deal with one of your hunts. You say how you know the founders, and you stay their point of contact.

Type it in. The company, where it is today, where it's going, and the round. Add the deck or other links if the founder is fine with the group seeing them.

Or let the founder pitch it. Send them a link. They pitch out loud on their own phone, AI drafts the deal from what they say, and you check every answer before you post.

There are no quotas: share a deal when you find one worth backing. Share only companies we'd be proud to back. That means no gambling, no adult content, and nothing built on cheating or deceiving people.

Founders also apply to Stag Hunt directly. Once deals are open for you, you can take the call on an application that fits your check, then share it as your own deal.

Why

This is the stag hunt itself. Sharing your best deal costs you little. When members can see who shares, cooperating tends to become the stable choice. A vouch from someone who knows the team does the work that weeks of meetings usually do, and their name on the record keeps the vouch honest.

Decide

6

Step 6. Make your call

On other members' deals, you answer one question: want to meet the team? Yes or no, with a line on why. You answer before you see anyone else's call, and your call locks once you submit it.

Invest

7

Step 7. Meet the team and invest directly

Want to meet the founders? Ask the member who brought the deal for an introduction. If you decide to invest, you do it directly with the company, in your own name, on its terms and documents. Stag Hunt never holds or moves money.

If the round is full, the founder picks who gets in. After you invest, log it on the deal's page.

Why

No one invests on your behalf, so no one else's incentives come between you and your money.

8

Step 8. Build your record

Every call you make is kept. When a company's outcome is known, your record shows which calls you got right: a yes on a company that did well, or a no on one that didn't. Your record is what other members look at.

Nobody can change it without it showing, Stag Hunt included, and you can download it any time.

Why

This is a repeated game. When calls are on the record, good judgment and honest sharing can build a reputation over time.

Apply

1

Step 1. Send your application

Fill in the application. It takes about 15 minutes. If the page offers it, you can pitch out loud to an AI interviewer instead: it fills in the form from what you say, and you check every answer before you send. If you don't send it, no member sees it.

Have these ready:

  • A one-minute video, shared as a link
  • Your numbers: customers, revenue, growth, what you charge
  • Your round: amount, terms, smallest check, closing date
  • Your pitch deck, if you have one

Members look for companies they'd be proud to back: no gambling, no adult content, and nothing built on cheating or deceiving people.

2

Step 2. Who can read it

Your application is not public. It goes to members who have stated in writing that they're accredited and will keep it confidential, and whose check size fits your round. Stag Hunt's admins can open any application.

Only the member who takes your call, and Stag Hunt's admins, see your email address. If you pitched out loud, the members who can read your application can also read the transcript and listen to the recording. The Privacy Policy has the details.

Talk

3

Step 3. A member may reach out

Members whose check size fits can read your application. If one wants to hear more, that member takes the call and reaches out to you. No one is required to.

Why

A real conversation can find what a form misses, in both directions.

Members decide

4

Step 4. They share your company with their hunt

If that member would back you, they share your company with their hunt: the group of investors they share deals with. The deal shows that you applied, and the member who took your call stays your point of contact.

5

Step 5. Each answer is made alone

Each member of that hunt whose check size fits can answer one question: want to meet the team? Each one answers before seeing anyone else's answer.

Why

Answering alone helps keep each view independent.

Raise

6

Step 6. Anyone who invests does it directly

Members who want to meet you ask for an introduction. Anyone who invests does it directly with your company, in their own name, on your terms and documents. If the round is full, you pick who gets in.

It costs nothing. Stag Hunt is not a fund and takes no fees. Applying is not a promise of a call or of investment.

Deal terms are not published, so your round stays yours to announce.

Why

Stag Hunt takes nothing from founders, and members may not accept anything from a company for bringing its deal.

Common questions

What does it cost?

Nothing, for investors or founders. No dues, no fees and no share of profits.

Does Stag Hunt hold or invest my money?

No. Stag Hunt is not a fund. It never pools, holds or moves money, and it never invests for anyone. Every investment is made directly between a member and a company, on that company's own terms and documents.

Do I have to be accredited to join?

No. Anyone can make an account. Other members' deals are shown only to members who say in writing that they're accredited. If you're not, you can still bring deals, start hunts and build your record.

Why do I wait 30 days before I see deals?

So that every round reaches only people Stag Hunt already knows. While you wait, you can bring deals, start hunts and fill in your profile.

Who can see a deal I share?

The members of the hunt you share it in, if they can see deals and their check size fits. Members outside that hunt don't.

Two exceptions. Observers, read-only accounts that every member can see listed, can read every deal. And an admin can open a deal that a member has reported. No deal is ever shown publicly.

Can I change my call?

No. A call locks when you submit it, so it stays your own read.

Is a yes a promise to invest?

No. A yes means you want to meet the founders. Whether you invest is your own decision, on your own clock.

How will I hear about new deals?

By email when a deal that fits you is shared, and in a digest every Monday. You can turn either one off in your profile.

Is any of this investment advice?

No. Members' calls and comments are opinions, not investment advice. Startup investing is illiquid and high-risk, and most startups fail. Invest only what you can afford to lose entirely.
